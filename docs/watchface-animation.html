Watch face layer engine: the design format

A manual for people (and AI sessions) who write Custom watch face designs for the personal build of AAPS. A design is a zip with one JSON file and some pictures. This page tells what the layer engine of the watch reads from the JSON: the animation, the looks and a few view keys.

About the pictures on this page. Every example has a small round picture. Your browser draws it with a small copy of the rules of the engine. The copy reads the same JSON text that you see beside the picture. A picture shows the idea. For the exact pixels, look at the watch or the phone preview. The pictures of the zip, the moon and the views of the face are drawn by the page itself and look different on the watch. The random numbers (r1 to r8) can differ from those on the watch. Click a picture to play it again. Click in the JSON of an example and type to change it: the picture follows, and the Reset button or a reload of the page brings the old text back. The Copy button copies the JSON as it is in the box.

1. Overview

A design can have a wake animation. It plays for a few seconds each time the watch wakes: when the face starts, when the watch comes out of the always-on screen, when the face becomes visible again, and when you tap the face (only while no animation plays). After that the face is still again and costs no extra battery.

The animation is the key animation at the top of the design JSON. It holds the list layers. A layer draws one thing: a picture, a simple shape, or a view of the face. Almost every number of a layer can be a formula of the time, so the thing moves, turns, grows or fades.

All places and sizes are in the design space of 400 x 400. The point 0, 0 is the top left corner, x goes right and y goes down. The middle of the face is 200, 200. The watch scales the design space to its own screen.

A broken animation does not break the rest of the design.

How a frame is built: slots

The face is drawn in parts, from the bottom to the top. A layer says with slot between which parts it is drawn. There are three slots: back (the default), views and front.

A wake frame

  1. Background picture and colour
  2. slot back: for example rain
  3. Complications, graph and data views (without the views that a view layer moves)
  4. slot views: the moved views, and shapes between them
  5. Clock text: time, hour, minute, second
  6. The rest of the data, the hour and minute hands
  7. slot front: for example a heart, a light
  8. Second hand

Read from the bottom (drawn first) to the top (drawn last).

A normal frame (no wake plays)

  1. Background picture and colour
  2. rest layers of slot back
  3. Complications, graph and data views
  4. rest layers of slot views
  5. Clock text
  6. The rest of the data, the hour and minute hands
  7. rest layers of slot front
  8. Second hand

Only layers with rest are drawn. This order holds while seconds are shown, see limits.

A view layer is always in the slot views, whatever it says.

Layers that stay: rest

A layer with "rest": <seconds> is also drawn in the normal frames, while no wake plays. It is drawn as it is that many seconds into a wake, with env = 0 and len = 30000. So a layer that multiplies its motion by env shows its still pose. rest is cut to 0 to 30. The values are read again for each normal frame.

A rest layer is a still picture. While seconds are shown, the face keeps its drawn parts for a whole minute (the top part for 10 seconds). So a rest layer that reads sec or min only moves once a minute. rest layers of pictures and shapes are not drawn on the always-on screen and not in the simple face. A view layer with rest places its view in every frame, also on the always-on screen.

2. Layer sources

Every layer has exactly one source: image, shape, text or view. A layer with none, or with two of them, is left out.

A picture of the zip: image

"image": "Heart" draws the zip file Heart.png (or .jpg, .svg). The key rect says where: left, top, width, height. Without rect the picture fills the whole face. A side that is not given takes the side of the whole face. The props x and y move the picture from its rect. A picture that is not in the zip leaves the layer out.

A picture of the app: "image": "@moon"

An image name that starts with @ is a picture that comes with the watch app, so the zip does not need it. There is one now: @moon, a photo of the full moon, cut round, with north up. It is only the photo: it shows no phase and it does not turn by itself. It is drawn in the middle of rect, as a square of the shorter side. It has no glow. The watch has no ready-made moon of this moment: a design builds it from this photo with plain layers. Chapter 13 does that step by step, and ends with a template you can copy.

A shape: shape

shape is "rect", "circle" or "line". Another word leaves the layer out. The colour comes from color, or from the list colors with the prop colorIndex.

A text: text

"text": "clock()" draws a text in one line. The value is a formula that gives a text, so a fixed text needs quotes inside the JSON text: "text": "'Hello'". A number is written as it is: "text": 42 shows 42. An empty text draws nothing.

The text is drawn at the exact place its formulas give, also at a part of a pixel. So two text layers with the same text, the same x, y, align and fontSize lie exactly on each other, also in two fonts whose signs are as wide. A view of the face cannot do that: it starts at a whole pixel.

"view": "sgv" takes a view that the face already has and moves, turns, scales, fades or cuts it. The name is the view key of the design, for example chart, sgv or freetext1. See view layers.

3. Layer keys

A layer has two kinds of keys. Props take a number or a formula text, and are worked out again for every frame and every copy. Fixed keys are read once, when the design is read. A fixed number may also be written as a formula of fixed numbers, like "maxScale": "1 + 0.2".

Mixing with what is below: blend

add and screen only make things lighter. Use them for light: a glow, an aurora, a lamp. add adds the colours and soon becomes white. screen is softer.

Layers on a sheet of their own: group

"group": "name" puts a layer in a group. Layers of one slot that follow each other with the same name are drawn together on a clear sheet of their own. Then the sheet is laid over the face. In a group two more values of blend work:

  • erase: the layer is not drawn. It takes away what the group has drawn so far, as much as the layer is solid. Its colour does not count.
  • inside: the layer is drawn only where the group has drawn something so far, as solid as that is, and it takes the place of what was there. It changes only the pixels in its own box: the rect of a picture, the outline of a rectangle or a circle.

Both change only the sheet of the group. The face below the group is never cut. A layer with erase or inside and no group is left out. A group ends at the first layer with another name or with none. A view layer is never in a group.

The sheet is as big as the clip.rect of the first layer of the group that is drawn. Without a clip.rect there it is as big as the face. A small sheet costs less, so give the first layer a clip.rect when the group is small. Nothing of the group shows outside its sheet.

A glow round a picture or a text: glow, glowAlpha

glow makes a soft shape of the picture in one colour, once, when the picture is made. The prop glowAlpha says how strong it shows; its default is 0, so a glow without glowAlpha shows nothing. A glow works on pictures of the zip and on texts: not on @moon, not on shapes, and not together with slices. Round a text the glow is a soft shadow of the signs, drawn with the text.

A colour that fades: gradient

"gradient": {"type": "linear" | "radial", "stops": [[position, alpha], ...]}. A stop says: at this position (0 to 1) the colour is this solid (0 to 1). There must be at least two stops, each a pair of numbers, or the layer is left out. linear runs along the width of a rectangle, from its left edge (0) to its right edge (1). On a circle it runs the same way, from the left edge of the circle to its right edge. radial runs from the middle of a circle (0) to its edge (1); on a rectangle it runs from the middle to the edges, stretched to the rectangle. A line has no gradient. A rectangle with a gradient needs a width above 0. Over a text, linear runs from the left end of the line to its right end, and radial in a circle from the middle of the line (0) to its ends (1).

The clip props

clipLeft, clipTop, clipRight and clipBottom are props, so they can move. A side that is not given stays open. They cut the layer more, on top of clip.

Sharp when big: maxScale

The watch makes the picture of a layer once, at the size of its rect times maxScale. When a layer scales its picture above 1, give the largest scale here, or the picture gets soft. A bigger picture costs more memory, so do not give more than needed.

4. Copies

A layer can be drawn many times. Give at most one of count, points and slices; with two of them the layer is left out. Every copy works out the formulas again with its own numbers: i (the number of the copy, from 0), n (how many copies) and u = i / (n - 1) (0 for the first copy, 1 for the last). One layer has at most 400 copies, all layers together 1500.

count and seed

"count": 60 draws the layer 60 times. Every copy gets its own random numbers r1 to r8, each from 0 to 1. They stay the same during the whole animation and at every wake. seed (default 1) chooses another set of random numbers.

points

"points": [[x, y], [x, y], ...] draws one copy for each point. The copy reads its point as px and py. A point may have up to 8 extra numbers: [x, y, p0, p1, ...]. The copy reads them as p0 to p7; an extra number that a point does not have is "no number". A point that does not start with two numbers is left out.

slices and slicesAlong

"slices": 80 cuts a picture into 80 strips, and each strip is a copy that can move on its own. Use it for a picture that waves, like an aurora or a flag. By default the strips stand up, side by side. With "slicesAlong": "y" they lie flat, one above the other. slices is cut to 1 to 200 and is only for pictures. A layer with slices has no glow.

5. Formulas

A prop is a number, or a text with a formula: "x": "200 + 110 * cos(t * 2)". A formula is math: it gives one number. It is read once and then worked out for every frame and every copy. A formula can also give a text, for a text layer: see texts in formulas.

  • Numbers: 12, 0.5, .5, 1e3. And pi.
  • Names: the variables, the values v_<key> and the names of a let. A name is made of letters, digits and _. An unknown name leaves the layer out.
  • Operators and brackets, see below.
  • Functions: name(value, value), see below. An unknown function, or a wrong number of values, leaves the layer out. A design can add its own functions.

True and false are numbers: a comparison gives 1 when it is true and 0 when it is not. Every number but 0 counts as true. A value that is missing is "no number". Math with no number gives no number, and a comparison with it is always false (also !=). A formula is at most 600 characters long, has at most 300 parts, and brackets and functions go at most 40 deep.

Named formulas: let

let gives a formula a name, so you write it once and use it many times. There are two places:

  • In animation: worked out once per frame, and every layer can use the name. Use only names that are the same for all copies here: t, ms, len, env, bpm, the clock, values and earlier names.
  • In a layer: worked out for every copy, before the props. It can use everything, also i, r1 or px.

The names are worked out in the order you write them, and each may use the names before it. A name is made of letters, digits and _ and does not start with a digit. It cannot be a variable, a function (also not a text function), pi or start with v_. A let holds a number, never a text. A layer may use a name again that the animation has: the one of the layer counts. There are at most 16 names in the animation and 16 in each layer. A name with a formula of fixed numbers costs nothing: the watch puts the number in its place.

Texts in formulas

A formula gives a number or a text. The watch knows which when it reads the formula, so a text where a number is needed is a mistake that leaves the layer out; it never shows up while a frame is drawn. The text of a text layer wants a text. Props and a let want a number, but they may use texts on the way: "visible": "len(clock()) == 5".

  • A fixed text stands between single quotes: 'hello'. Double quotes work too, but in JSON they need a backslash. The quote twice is one quote: 'it''s'.
  • a & b joins two texts: 'IOB ' & fixed(v_iob, 2). It comes after + and - and before the compares, as in a spreadsheet: 1 + 2 & 3 + 4 is 37.
  • A number where a text is wanted is written like a spreadsheet does by itself: a whole number without decimals, else at most 6 decimals, with the decimal sign of the watch language. A value that is missing gives an empty text. Use fixed or text to say how a number looks.
  • == and != compare two texts sign by sign; 'a' == 'A' is 0. < and > do not work on texts. A text and a number cannot be compared.
  • if(c, a, b) takes two numbers or two texts.
  • + does not join texts. 'a' + 'b' is a mistake.

Places in a text count from 1, as in a spreadsheet. A text is at most 200 signs long; a longer one is cut. Every number is written with the signs of the language of the watch (a comma or a dot, its digits).

Your own functions: functions

functions in animation gives a formula a name and values, so you write it once and call it with other values each time. The key is the name with its values in brackets, the text is the formula:

"functions": {
  "two(n)": "text(n, '00')",
  "swing(a, speed)": "a * sin(t * speed) * env",
  "bg()": "glucose(v_sgv)"
}
  • A function works in every formula of the animation: props, text, let, and other functions.
  • It gives a number or a text, as its formula does. One function can do both: "pick(c, a, b)": "if(c, a, b)" works with two numbers and with two texts.
  • The formula may use its values, the variables, the values v_, the names of the let of the animation (those written before the formula that calls the function) and other functions. It does not see the let of a layer: pass such a name as a value.
  • Inside the formula a value hides a variable of the same name: in "two(n)", n is the value, not the number of copies.
  • A function without values still needs its brackets: bg(). That is the way to give a text a name, because a let holds numbers only.
  • The name follows the rules of a let name, and cannot be the name of a function the watch already has. A name that comes twice keeps the first.
  • A function may call other functions, at most 4 deep, and never itself, also not through another one.
  • There are at most 32 functions, with at most 8 values each. The formula is at most 600 characters long.

A call costs nothing extra: when the watch reads a formula it puts the formula of the function in its place, with the values filled in. So the parts of the function count for the 300 parts of the formula that calls it, and the formula is worked out only as often as the values need (see what costs time). A function with a wrong head is left out. A mistake in the formula of a function leaves out the layers that call it; a function nobody calls is not checked.

The sun and the moon

The sky values follow the day and night rule of the watch. With a place they follow the real sun and moon there. For example in Amsterdam (52.37, 4.90) on a day in June the sun is up from about 05:20 to 22:00, so sun_progress is 0.5 at about 13:40. The place of the wearer: needsPlace

The sun, the moon and the weather need to know where the wearer is. The phone gives the place only for a design that needs it, and only when the wearer allows the phone's location. The phone finds out by itself when it reads the design. A design needs the place when it has one of these:

  • a formula anywhere in the JSON (a prop, a let, the when of a look) that reads v_sun_progress, v_night_progress, v_sun_cycle, v_moon_cycle, v_moon_limb, v_moon_north, v_sun_next_time or v_is_day (the sun and the moon);
  • a valueKey that is one of those keys (without is_day);
  • a formula that reads v_weather_kind or v_weather_temperature, or a valueKey weather_temperature (the weather: the phone looks it up and sends only the weather);
  • "needsPlace": true at the top of the JSON, for a design that wants the place for another reason.
{
  "needsPlace": true
}

Without a place a design must still look right: the day is then from 07:00 to 19:00 (or the fixed times of the wearer), the moon stands north up, and sun_next_time has no value. moon_phase, moon_lit and the photo @moon never need the place.

7. Templates: use

A template is an effect with parameters: one or more layers that a design can use more than once. The design keeps its templates in animation.templates, by name. An entry of layers with use is replaced by the layers of that template when the design is read:

{"use": "rain", "count": 120, "color": "#7FB6FF"}

The app has no ready-made templates. A design brings every template it uses. A use of a name that is not in the templates of the design is left out, with an error. The nine templates at the end of this chapter are ready to copy into a design.

The other keys of the entry are the parameters. A parameter that you do not give has its default. A number parameter is cut to its limits. A key that the template does not know is an error in the design tests, and is not used. So you cannot add layer keys such as slot or rest to a use entry: change the template for that.

How to write a template

A template in animation.templates has two keys:

  • params: every parameter with its default. The default is a number, a text, an object or a list. To give limits, write {"default": 40, "min": 0, "max": 200, "int": true}: the value is then cut to min and max, and made a whole number (cut towards 0) with "int": true. A parameter with limits must be a number. A limit may be a formula of the parameters before it, like "max": "$periodMs".
  • layers: the layers, with the parameters written as $name.

How $name is filled in:

  • A text that is exactly "$name" becomes the value itself, of any type: "count": "$count" becomes "count": 40, and "rect": "$area" becomes the whole object.
  • $name inside a longer text becomes the value as text. A number is written plain, and in brackets when it is negative, so it can stand anywhere in a formula: "$size * (0.5 + r5)" becomes "3 * (0.5 + r5)".
  • $name.key is one entry of an object parameter: $area.left.
  • When the design gives an object for an object parameter, it is laid over the default key by key. So "area": {"top": 200} keeps the other sides.
  • A layer of a template with "if": "<formula of the parameters>" is left out when the formula is 0. After the parameters are filled in, it must be a formula of fixed numbers.
  • A layer of a template may use another template of the design, at most 4 deep, and never itself.

8. View layers

A layer with "view": "<view key>" changes a view that the face already has: the graph, a number, a hand, a free text. The view keeps its own content; the layer only says where and how it is drawn.

How a moved view is drawn

While a wake plays, a view with a view layer is taken out of its normal part of the face and drawn in the slot views: above the other data and below the clock text. View layers that follow each other in the list are one group; the views of a group are drawn in the paint order of the face. A shape or picture layer with "slot": "views" between two view layers splits them into two groups, so it lies between those views.

Views to leave alone. The clock text (time, hour, minute, second) and second_hand are drawn at their own place in every frame. A view layer that moves one of them in a wake shows it twice. The background is not a view of the layout: a view layer on it does nothing. A view layer for a view that the face does not have does nothing. Redrawing a view every frame costs time, so move few views, and small ones.

A view layer with rest: a place for every frame

A view layer with rest also places its view in the normal frames, and on the always-on screen. This is how a design puts a view on a place that follows a value: a sun that goes over the face by day, a cloud that follows the moon. The watch works the formulas out with t = rest and env = 0, each time the face is drawn new (when data comes in, and once a minute). In this case rot0 is the turn of the design itself, and dx0 is 0.

While a wake plays, the layer gives only the change from its rest place. A design that has only view layers with rest whose formulas do not read the time has no wake to play.

9. Looks

A look changes the design while a condition is true: other colours by day, another background in the rain. The looks are the list looks at the top of the design JSON, beside animation.

10. Keys outside animation

These keys belong to the same personal engine, but they are not in animation. The upstream view keys of a Custom watch face (width, topmargin, fontColor, dynData, ...) are not part of this manual.

A picture on a view: image

A view can show a picture with the view key image. It is one of three things: the name of a zip picture, an app picture ("@moon", the photo of the full moon), or a template with its parameters: {"use": "moon"}. The picture is the background of the view, stretched to its size, and it is coloured like the key background. Looks can swap it. It works on text views (such as freetext1 to freetext4), not on picture views.

The template is one of animation.templates of the design. Its layers are drawn on a square picture as big as the shorter side of the view. The whole 400 x 400 design space of the layers is that square, so a template that fills the design space fills the view. Make the view a square. The layers are drawn still, as layers with rest 0 are, with the values of now. The picture is made again only when one of the values it reads changes. View layers of the template are left out, and a picture is at most 512 pixels a side.

A moment as a text: @time

The text of a view with dynData is a number format, like "%.0f%%". For a value that is a moment (sun_next_time), write @time in the text: the watch puts the clock time there, in the 12 or 24 hour form of the watch.

The glucose as a line: graphLineWidth, graphLineColor

The graph draws the glucose as loose dots. With graphLineWidth (a whole number, at most 8, at the top of the JSON) it draws a line of that width instead. Each part of the line keeps the colour of its range (low, in range, high); the colour changes half way between two readings. graphLineColor gives the whole line one colour; it stands beside the other graph colours (at the top of the JSON, or in the colour block of a dynPref). The line breaks where readings are missing, and the newest reading gets a dot.

Colours the wearer can choose: colourLooks

A design can offer one or two colours that the wearer chooses, on the watch or in the AAPS app ("Main colour" for colour1, "Second colour" for colour2). Each is a patch, as in a look. In it every text that ends with @colour becomes the chosen colour: "@colour" becomes for example "#00E5FF", and "#B0@colour" becomes "#B000E5FF", the same colour a little clear. While the wearer leaves the setting at "as designed", nothing changes. The colours are laid over the design before the looks, so a look can still change them.

Choices the wearer can make: options

A design can offer up to four options, for example "Santa: at Christmas, always, never". The list options is at the top of the design JSON. The watch and the AAPS app show one row per option, with the title and the choices of the design. 11. Limits and tips

Keep the zip small: about 320 KB. A bigger design never reaches the watch, and there is no error. The files travel unpacked, so the sizes of the files count too. A text follows the same table, and goes one step further. The watch keeps the values a text formula read and the text they gave, for each copy. When the values are the same again it uses the kept text: nothing is worked out and no new text is made. Inside a formula each text function keeps its own result too, so text(floor(t), '0') makes a new text once a second, not in every frame, and clock() once a minute. The width of a line is measured only for a new text. So a text that does not change costs one draw call per frame.

  • Keep the per-copy formulas short. 300 copies at 24 frames per second are 7200 sums per second for each prop. Put the part that is the same for all copies in a let: a let that reads only the time is worked out once per frame.
  • Hide with visible. A layer whose visible is false, or whose alpha is 0.002 or less for all copies, costs nothing in that frame: the watch does not touch the screen for it. Give a layer that only shows for a second a visible with its time window.
  • A layer without rotation and scale is cheaper. Do not give them when you do not need them.
  • A mask is the most costly clip. A layer with a mask is drawn on its own first, in every frame. Keep that area small with maskRect or clip.rect.
  • Pictures are made once. A picture is made at the size of its rect times maxScale, and its glow too. Two layers that show the same picture at the same size share it. Drawing a picture in a frame is cheap.
  • Layers in a row share the round cut. A layer with a clip.rect between them breaks that row, so keep such layers together.
  • Fewer frames. A wake draws durationMs x fps / 1000 frames. A slow effect looks fine at 15 frames per second.
  • Layers with rest are nearly free. In the normal frames they are drawn into the kept pictures of the face: once a minute (the slots back and views) or every 10 seconds (the slot front).
  • Move few views. A view with a view layer is drawn again in every frame of the wake. A big view, like the graph, costs the most.
  • A group is drawn on a sheet of its own first, in every frame it shows. Keep the sheet small with a clip.rect on its first layer. The moon template of chapter 13 does that.

Known limits

  • rest layers of the slots views and front lie between the right parts of the face only while seconds are shown. When the watch shows no seconds, the face is drawn as a whole and these layers are drawn over the whole face, also over the second hand.
  • A rest layer does not move with the time: one that reads sec or min changes once a minute at most.
  • A view layer with rest moves its view in steps of whole screen pixels.
  • The view key image works on text views only.
  • The random numbers of a layer without count change when a layer is added before it.
  • The values are read at the start of a wake. A value that changes while the wake plays shows in the next wake.
  • A text layer writes one line. For more lines use more layers.
  • A text that reads the clock (clock(), date()) in a template that is the picture of a view does not follow the time: that picture is made again only when a value changes.
  • When only the font file of a design changes and nothing else, the text layers keep the old font until the design is loaded again.
  • On this page a font of a design zip is drawn in a font of your browser, text(x, format) knows only the format signs of its table, and date only the letters of its table. The watch knows all of the Java formats.
  • The variable ms will go away later. Use t * 1000 in new designs when you can.

13. Example: the moon from plain layers

The watch has no drawing code for the moon, and no ready-made moon. A design draws the moon of this moment with six plain layers: the photo @moon two times, and four circles. This chapter builds them one by one, and ends with the same layers as a template to copy into your design. Three values say how the moon looks now: v_moon_lit (how much is lit), v_moon_limb (where the lit side points) and v_moon_north (how the moon is turned), see chapter 6.

Every picture has two sliders. "Moon phase" sets how much is lit, and on which side. "Turn in the sky" turns the moon, as the place and the time of day do on the watch. The page has no photo: it draws a simple full moon with a white spot at its north.

Step 1: the photo, turned

@moon is the full moon with north up. The value v_moon_north is the turn of the moon in the sky, in the same degrees as rotation.

Step 2: the lit half

Now the shape of the lit part, in white, without the photo. Start with the half of the disc on the side of the sun. A linear gradient on a circle runs from its left edge to its right edge. With these stops the left half is clear and the right half is solid, with a soft line between them. v_moon_limb is the direction of the lit side, counted from straight up, and the solid half points to the right. So the circle is turned by v_moon_limb - 90.

Step 3: more than half, or less

The line between light and dark on a ball looks like half an ellipse. A circle with a scaleX is an ellipse. At full moon and at new moon it is as wide as the disc, at a quarter it has no width: its half width is the radius times 2 * v_moon_lit - 1, without the sign. When more than half is lit, the ellipse is added to the half disc. When less than half is lit, it is taken away from it, with "blend": "erase". That needs a group, so the three shapes get one. The key visible picks which of the two ellipses is used. The first layer of the group gives a clip.rect, so the sheet of the group is only as big as the moon.

Step 4: the photo inside the shape

The last layer of the group is the photo with "blend": "inside". It is drawn only where the group has drawn the white shape, and it takes the place of the white. maxScale 2 asks for the photo at twice its size, so it stays sharp when it is turned.

Step 5: the dark part

The dark part of the real moon is not black. You can still see it at night, and by day the blue of the sky shows through it. So below the group come two faint layers that fill the whole disc: a black circle and the photo. With how bright the dark part is (dark, 0.3) and how much it covers (cover, 0.45), the photo gets the alpha dark * cover and the black circle 1 - (1 - cover) / (1 - dark * cover). The lit part of step 4 is drawn over them and covers them.

Step 6: the moon as a template to copy

The same six layers as a template of the design, with parameters where step 5 has numbers. Copy the entry "moon" into the animation.templates of your design. Then one line draws the moon: {"use": "moon", "left": 110, "top": 110, "size": 180}. A view can show it too, with "image": {"use": "moon"}, see image on a view. The watch does not know this template by itself: a design that uses it must bring it.

The layers of the template have "clip": {"round": false}, so the moon is not cut to the round screen when it is the picture of a view. The formulas read v_moon_limb and v_moon_north, so a design with this template needs the place, see needsPlace.
